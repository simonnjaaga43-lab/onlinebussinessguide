How to Create a Website for a Small Business in Kenya: A Beginner's Guide

Starting a small business is one thing. Making it easy for people to discover that business online is another.

You might already be selling clothes through WhatsApp, promoting cakes on Facebook, offering professional services through referrals, or running a shop that depends on customers walking through the door.

These methods can help you find customers. But what happens when someone searches for your business on Google and cannot find a proper website?

A website gives your business a place online where potential customers can learn about what you offer, understand how to reach you, view your products or services, and decide whether they want to work with you.

The good news is that you don't need to be a professional programmer to create one.

In this guide, we'll explain how to create a website for a small business in Kenya, what you need before starting, which platforms you can consider, and how to launch a website that serves your customers.

1. Understand what you want your website to do

Before choosing a platform or paying for hosting, decide what purpose your website will serve.

A website should solve a business problem, not simply exist because other businesses have one.

For example:

  • A clothing business may need a website where customers can view products, prices and ordering instructions.
  • A bakery may need a gallery of cakes, customer reviews and an enquiry button.
  • A carpenter may need a portfolio showing completed furniture and installation projects.
  • A consultant may need a website explaining their services and allowing customers to request appointments.

Start by identifying your main goal.

Do you want customers to call you? Send WhatsApp messages? Request quotations? View your products? Make purchases?

Your answer will influence the type of website you need.

2. Prepare the information your website needs

One common mistake beginners make is starting website development before preparing the business information.

This can make the process confusing and unnecessarily expensive.

Prepare the following:

Business name: Use the name customers recognize.

Business description: Explain what your business does, who it serves and what makes its products or services useful.

Products or services: List what you offer. Include prices where appropriate.

Contact information: Prepare your business phone number, email address and location if customers visit your premises.

Business photographs: Use clear, genuine photographs of your products, work or business premises.

Brand identity: Prepare your logo, preferred colours and other visual elements if available.

You don't need everything to be perfect before starting. However, having these details ready will make building your website easier.

3. Choose how you want to build your website

There are several ways to create a website. The right choice depends on your budget, technical confidence, time and business needs.

Let's examine the main options.

Option A: WordPress

WordPress is a popular website publishing platform that allows users to create websites, blogs and online stores.

With self-hosted WordPress, you install the WordPress software on a hosting account that you control.

You can use themes to determine how your website looks and plugins to add functionality.

For example, a small business could use WordPress to create:

  • A homepage introducing the business.
  • A services page explaining what it offers.
  • A gallery displaying completed work.
  • A blog publishing useful information.
  • A contact page with enquiry options.

Who should consider it?

Business owners who want flexibility, room to expand and more control over their website.

Keep in mind that self-hosted WordPress requires hosting, and you are responsible for keeping the installation, themes and plugins updated.

Option B: Website builders

Website builders allow users to create websites using visual editing tools, templates and ready-made sections.

You generally don't need to write code to create a basic website.

Examples include Wix and Squarespace.

Who should consider them?

Beginners who want a relatively straightforward building experience and prefer managing their website through a visual interface.

However, check the subscription costs, renewal prices, available features and payment integrations before choosing a platform.

Option C: Build a simple website using HTML and CSS

HTML and CSS are technologies used to create and style websites.

HTML provides the structure, while CSS controls the appearance.

You can use them to build a simple website with pages such as Home, About, Services and Contact.

This approach offers flexibility and can be inexpensive, especially when you already have access to suitable free hosting.

However, it requires learning some technical skills. More advanced features, such as online payments and customer accounts, require additional development.

Who should consider it?

People interested in learning web development or businesses that need a simple website and are comfortable with a more hands-on approach.

Option D: Hire a website developer

You don't have to build the website yourself.

You can hire a freelancer or web development company to design and build it.

This can save time, especially if your business needs a more advanced website.

Before hiring anyone, prepare a clear description of what you need. Ask for a breakdown of the work, delivery timeline, maintenance arrangements and total cost.

Most importantly, make sure the domain name and hosting accounts are registered in your business's name, and that you receive the necessary access credentials and website files.

4. Choose a domain name

A domain name is your website's address on the internet.

For example:

www.yourbusiness.co.ke

It is what customers type into their browsers to find your website.

When choosing a domain name, consider the following:

  • Keep it short and easy to remember.
  • Make it relevant to your business.
  • Avoid unnecessary numbers and complicated spelling.
  • Choose a name that customers can easily pronounce.
  • Check whether the name is available before committing to it.

For a business serving customers in Kenya, a .co.ke domain may communicate a local business presence.

A .com domain can also work well, particularly if you plan to serve customers internationally.

You can compare domain registration options through providers and check .ke domain information through the Kenya Network Information Centre (KeNIC).

Remember that domain registration is normally renewed periodically. Check both the initial registration price and the renewal price before purchasing.

5. Understand website hosting

A domain name gives people an address to visit. Hosting provides the space and infrastructure that make your website accessible online.

Think of it this way:

  • Your domain is the address.
  • Your hosting is where your website's files are stored.
  • Your website is the actual content visitors see.

Some website builders include hosting in their subscription plans.

With self-hosted WordPress or a custom HTML website, you generally arrange hosting separately.

When comparing hosting providers, consider:

Reliability: Your website should be accessible when customers need it.

Security: Look for SSL support, backups and appropriate security features.

Storage: Choose a plan that can accommodate your website's files and images.

Support: Check whether assistance is available when something goes wrong.

Renewal costs: Don't choose a service based only on its introductory offer.

For a simple business website, basic shared hosting may be sufficient. You don't necessarily need an expensive hosting package when starting.

6. Create the essential pages

A small business website doesn't need dozens of pages.

Start with the pages that help customers understand your business and take action.

Homepage

This is usually the first page visitors see.

It should quickly explain:

  • What your business does.
  • Who your products or services are for.
  • What customers should do next.

Use a clear headline, a short description and a visible contact or enquiry option.

About page

Introduce your business.

Explain what you do, why you started and what customers can expect when working with you.

You don't need a long personal story. Focus on information that helps visitors understand and trust your business.

Products or Services page

Explain what you sell or the services you provide.

Use clear descriptions, relevant photographs and prices where appropriate.

Help customers understand what they are getting before contacting you.

Contact page

Make it easy for people to reach you.

Include your business phone number, email address, location where relevant and a WhatsApp contact option.

Frequently Asked Questions

This page is optional, but it can be useful.

Answer questions customers regularly ask about pricing, delivery, availability, booking, payments or service areas.

You can add more pages as your business grows.

7. Make it easy for customers to contact you

A website should not leave visitors wondering what to do next.

Imagine someone visiting your bakery's website. They like the cakes but cannot find a phone number or ordering instructions.

They may simply leave.

Make your contact options visible.

For example, you can include:

  • A WhatsApp enquiry button.
  • A click-to-call phone number.
  • A business email address.
  • A contact form.
  • A physical business location where relevant.

If customers place orders through WhatsApp, make that process clear.

You can also use WhatsApp Business to organize customer conversations, display your business information and manage product catalogues.

For businesses that accept online payments, investigate suitable payment providers and their requirements before integrating them.

For example, Safaricom's Daraja platform provides APIs for businesses and developers who want to integrate M-Pesa services into applications. Payment integration requires appropriate setup and testing; simply displaying an M-Pesa number is not the same as building an automated checkout.

8. Make your website mobile-friendly

Many customers will visit your website using a smartphone.

A website that looks good on a laptop but is difficult to use on a phone can frustrate potential customers.

Check that:

  • Text is readable without zooming.
  • Buttons are easy to tap.
  • Images fit the screen.
  • Menus work properly.
  • Pages load reasonably quickly.
  • Contact details are easy to find.

Test your website on an actual phone before announcing it to customers.

You don't need complicated animations or excessive design features. A clean, simple and easy-to-use website is often a better starting point.

9. Help customers find your website on Google

Creating a website is only part of the process. People also need ways to discover it.

Search engine optimization (SEO) involves improving your website so search engines can understand its content and potentially show it to relevant searchers.

Start with these practices:

Use descriptive page titles. Each page should have a title that explains what it contains.

Write useful content. Explain your products, services and areas of expertise clearly.

Use relevant keywords naturally. If you operate a bakery in Kisii, for example, your website should make it clear that you offer bakery services in Kisii. Avoid repeating keywords unnaturally.

Use descriptive headings. Organize information into sections that are easy to understand.

Optimize images. Use suitable image sizes and descriptive alternative text.

Create a Google Search Console account. It helps you monitor how Google discovers and understands your website. Visit Google Search Console.

If your business serves customers at a physical location or within a defined service area, investigate Google Business Profile eligibility and setup.

SEO takes time. Publishing a website does not guarantee that it will immediately appear in search results.

Focus on creating helpful pages and making your business information clear and accurate.

10. Understand the costs before you start

How much does it cost to create a website in Kenya?

There is no single price because the cost depends on the platform, website features, hosting, domain and whether you build it yourself or hire someone.

Here are the main expenses to consider:

Expense What it covers
Domain registration Your website address
Hosting Keeping your website online
Website platform Building and managing your website
Design and development Professional setup, if outsourced
Business email Professional email address
Maintenance Updates, backups and technical support
Additional features Online payments, booking systems or e-commerce

A basic website that you build yourself can cost considerably less than a professionally developed website.

However, free website tools may have limitations, such as platform branding, restricted features or a platform-owned web address.

A professional website can cost more depending on the number of pages, custom features, content and support included.

For that reason, don't compare website prices without comparing what each package actually provides.

Ask these questions before paying:

  1. Is the domain included?
  2. Is hosting included, and for how long?
  3. What is the renewal price?
  4. Who owns the domain and website files?
  5. Are mobile-friendly design and basic SEO included?
  6. Will I receive access to update my website?
  7. What happens when I need technical support?

A cheap offer is not automatically a good deal, and an expensive package is not automatically the right choice.

Choose according to what your business actually needs.

11. Common mistakes to avoid

Before launching your website, watch out for these mistakes.

Building without a clear purpose: Decide what you want visitors to do before designing the pages.

Choosing a complicated domain: A difficult website address can be hard for customers to remember.

Ignoring mobile users: Always test the website on a phone.

Using unclear descriptions: Customers should understand what you offer without guessing.

Overloading the homepage: Avoid filling it with unnecessary text, animations and decorative elements.

Forgetting contact information: Make it easy for potential customers to reach you.

Ignoring recurring costs: Remember that domains, hosting and some website platforms require renewal payments.

Giving away ownership: Keep control of your domain, hosting accounts and important website files.

Expecting immediate traffic: A website needs useful content, visibility and ongoing improvement to attract visitors.

12. Your website launch checklist

Before announcing your website, go through this checklist:

  • The website clearly explains what the business offers.
  • The domain name is correct.
  • The homepage works properly.
  • Products or services are clearly described.
  • Contact details are accurate.
  • WhatsApp or other enquiry options work.
  • The website displays properly on a smartphone.
  • Images load correctly.
  • All navigation links work.
  • The website uses HTTPS.
  • Important pages have descriptive titles.
  • Google Search Console has been set up.

You don't have to make everything perfect before launching. Start with a functional website and improve it as you learn from your customers.

Conclusion: Start with what your business needs

Creating a website for a small business in Kenya doesn't have to be complicated.

You can start with a simple website that explains what you offer, shows customers what you do and makes it easy for them to contact you.

You don't need dozens of pages, expensive animations or advanced technology to make your first website useful.

Start by understanding your business needs. Choose a suitable platform, prepare your content, create the essential pages and make sure customers can reach you.

Then keep improving.

Your website should grow alongside your business.

The goal is not simply to have a website. The goal is to build an online presence that helps your business become easier to discover, understand and trust.
